yeti-code is an agentic AI students learn with — and that gives you a complete record of every prompt, tool call, and decision they made.

yeti-code — live agent 

      


      

        A real session, looped. One leader. Four workers in parallel.
        Every step is logged.
      

    


    
    

      
The problem

      
Your students are already using AI.
You can't see how.

      

        The course allows it. Good — that matches reality. But you see only
        the submitted notebook. You don't see the dead ends, the prompt
        iterations, the moments where understanding clicked or didn't.
        You're grading the answer, not the work.
      

      

        ChatGPT is a black box.
yeti-code is a glass box.
      

    


    
    

      
Why now

      
One-shot prompts are a 2023 skill.

      

        Every dev tool that matters in 2026 — Claude Code, Cursor, Devin,
        Aider — is a tool-calling agent loop. The model plans,
        calls a tool, reads the result, self-corrects, and tries again.
        Students who graduate knowing only ChatGPT are graduating with
        yesterday's skill set.
      

      

        yeti-code teaches the new pattern by letting students live in
        it for an entire semester.
      

    


    
    

      
What changes

      
Two halves of the same idea.


      

        

          
For students

          
An agent they can read.

          

            Every tool call is named, every argument visible, every result
            in plain text. Multi-agent team mode shows four workers run in
            parallel. Plan-and-execute shows a planner think out loud
            before an executor acts.
          

          

            They graduate fluent in the patterns industry runs on:
            tool selection, delegation, parallel work, prompt switching.
            Not as theory — as muscle memory.
          

        


        

          
For you

          
Every step, on disk.

          

            A timestamped log of every session lands in
            ~/.yeti-code/logs/. Prompts, tool calls, model
            switches, errors, parallel-execution speedups. Local only —
            students decide what to share.
          

          

            Grade the process, not just the output. Spot the
            student who shipped without understanding, and the one who
            quietly mastered something deep.
          

        

      

    


    
    

      
What's next

      
Today they run agents.
Next term they build them.

      

        The next step is letting students author their own
        multi-step workflows — declarative files, runnable with one
        command, gradeable as artefacts. Six lessons that take them from
        understanding the agent loop to shipping a workflow as their
        capstone.
      

      

        The loop closes: build, run, trace, reflect, iterate.
        That's how a teaching tool becomes a teaching method.
